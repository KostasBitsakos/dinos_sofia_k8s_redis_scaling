## 0. What stage A showed (facts used for the design)
- c=1, 200m limit, uniform keys, 95/5 GET/SET, 2000 x 64 B: probe `capacity_est` 101 506 ops/s; the sweep served up to 118 037 ops/s
  (probe under-estimates by about 13 %). Pod CPU was at the 200m quota from about 3 000 ops/s upward (`pods_busy` 0.91 at 3.2k,
  about 1.0 from 13k): CPU per op falls with load (batching), so CPU is NOT linear in throughput; p99 was 70-170 ms at 30-100k ops/s
  (compatible with CFS throttling at a 100 ms period; a hypothesis, tested here by comparing the 200m and 50m namespaces).
- The sweep gave no capacity (`check_sweep.py`): only one level had served/offered < 0.95 with busy pods. Cause: fractions stopped at
  1.5 and the 1.2 level served 0.969. Fix: fraction 2.0 added (default of `run_c.py`) so >= 2 deeply saturated levels exist.
- Generator budget: about 3.8 host cores for 100k ops/s (38 us/op), busiest worker 0.49 core at 12.5k ops/s each. A worker is invalid above
  0.85 core, i.e. above about 22.4k offered ops/s; 16 workers can validly drive at most `common.gen_budget(16)` = 358k ops/s (host invalid above 60 % of 40 cpus).
  A saturation bracket needs offered load >= 1.2 x capacity. 200m, c=1 (about 100-118k): 1.2x = 140k, fits 8 workers. 200m, c=2 (about 200-236k): 1.2x = 283k fits 16 workers,
  but the 2.0 level (above ~358k) does not. **200m, c=4 (about 400-470k, 1.2x = 480-560k) cannot be bracketed within the budget and is DROPPED from the 200m namespace**
  (it could only measure the generator); c >= 8 at 200m likewise. This is why the second namespace exists. Enforcement: `run_c.py` never runs a fraction whose
  offered rate exceeds `gen_budget(workers)` (event `fraction_skipped_gen_budget`; `gen_budget_no_overload` when no level reaches 1.2 x), `run_transition.py` clamps its rate
  to the budget (logged `CLAMPED`; fit.py then censors the unsaturated window). The analysis rejects generator-limited levels (lag > 50 ms, worker > 0.85 core, host > 60 %) before any capacity
  is computed (test `test_generator_saturated_levels_never_give_capacity`); a plateau is accepted as capacity only if the pods' own CPU is at the limit.
  The 50m per-pod capacity (assumed about 25k ops/s) is UNMEASURED: if it is much higher (batching raised 200m efficiency with load), c=8 may exceed the budget and be unbracketable; the log shows it.
- Stage A c=2 failure: run `20261017-000209-R-20261016-022-e4-a1`: 1 s after the replica scale-up the memory watch tried to read the new,
  not-yet-Ready pod (no cgroup, `kubectl top` has no sample yet), recorded `memory_abort why=unreadable`, scaling stopped. A code bug, not a cluster fault; entries 05-14 then
  failed by gating and entry 15 rolled back. Fixed in commit 0c62cf4 (only Ready pods older than 15 s are watched; unit-tested).

**Acceptance rule (preregistered, `fit_extra.substitution_check`, `fit.py --substitution FIT_200M FIT_50M`):** with r_c = C200(c)/C50(c) over the pod counts bracketed in both
namespaces (c = 1 and 2; c = 4 cannot be measured at 200m, see section 0), the substitution is accepted iff every r_c is within +-15 % of the mean ratio (the same 15 % that sweeps must agree by).
With only two common c this is a weak test; it is reported with the numbers, and "not accepted" or "undetermined" is a legitimate outcome.
It validates capacity SCALING with the pod CPU limit only. It does NOT validate latency tails (CFS throttling periods are the same 100 ms but the quota is 4x smaller), pod start-up, migration, the CPU
mapping or memory. The 200m and 50m calibrations are kept separate and labelled; transition (delta, k), start-up and tail parameters measured at 50m must be
re-validated by at least one production-limit (200m) transition run (c = 2 <-> 3) before being transferred to the production-limit Sim v2 parameters. That run is NOT in this spec. `fit.py --pod-cpu-limit 0.05` (or `QRL_POD_CPU_LIMIT`) for 50m runs; never fit both together.

## 2. Measurements, entries, and which model change they settle
Definitions: throughput = server-side served ops. **SLO-feasible throughput** (`fit_extra.slo_feasible`, per c) = largest served ops/s of a valid level (generator, host and telemetry rules of fit.py)
whose drops, timeouts and errors are each <= 0.1 % of offered AND whose p95 <= threshold, for thresholds {10, 50, 100, 250} ms (the reward SLO of section 2b is a separate, narrower measurement). It is reported next to the
saturation capacity C(c) (bracket rule of PLAN.md section 6: >= 1 sub-saturation level with served/offered >= 0.97 and >= 2 saturated levels with busy pods agreeing within 10 %; >= 2 sweeps within 15 %).
Fractions of the probed capacity: 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 0.95, 1.05, 1.2, 1.5, 2.0 (2.0 = overload), 30 s per level (first 10 s warm); levels above the generator budget are not run.
Every level carries generator lag, busiest-worker cores, host busy fraction, and **CFS throttling** (`telemetry.jsonl`: `cfs_quota_us`, `cfs_period_us`, and per 2 s `d_nr_periods`, `d_nr_throttled`, `d_throttled_s`, summed over pods, from the
pods' `cpu.stat`); `fit.level_table` derives `throttled_frac` (share of CFS periods throttled). A level with `throttled_frac` > 10 % is marked throttling-affected: its p95 is interpreted as a property of the CPU quota.

## 2b. Reward SLO (researcher decision R-20261017-018)
Definition: `slo_s = 2 x p95 latency of ONE pod (c=1, production 200m limit) at half of its measured saturation throughput`. **Interpretation (ours; the researcher's wording is "50 % offered load"):** the OFFERED load equals
0.5 x C(1), C(1) = bracketed saturation throughput from `sweep-200m-c1-a/-b` (median, 2 sweeps within 15 %). The alternative reading (half of an SLO-feasible throughput) is circular and not used. Researcher to confirm.
Dedicated entries `slo-200m-c1-r1..r3`: `run_c.py --c 1 --capacity-auto --tag 200m --fractions 0.4,0.5,0.575,0.65 --level-s 130` (120 valid seconds per level; the ladder brackets 0.5 x C(1) if the probe is 0-25 % low; stage A: 13 % low).
`slo_candidate.py --sweeps <c=1 sweep runs> --slo-runs <r1 r2 r3> --out slo_candidate.json`:
- p95 is interpolated (log-linear) against the **offered** rate between the two valid levels of a repetition that bracket 0.5 x C(1);
- a repetition counts only if both bracketing levels are valid by fit.py and have drops, timeouts and errors each <= 0.1 % of offered; **at least 3 distinct valid repetitions are required, otherwise `slo_s` is null** (tested: one or two valid runs give null);
- uncertainty `slo_s_ci95_total` = union of the 95 % t-intervals (df = 2) across repetitions, recomputed at the minimum, median and maximum of the two C(1) sweeps (capacity uncertainty), widened by +-2.5 % (half a 5 % histogram bucket);
- `deployment_specific` = true if more than 10 % of CFS periods were throttled at the used levels, or throttling data is missing: then p95 reflects the 200m CPU quota (stage A showed CPU at the quota from about 3 000 ops/s) and the SLO must be reported as a property of this pod limit, not of Redis.
Output keys: `slo_s`, `slo_s_ci95_total`, `p95_ms_mean`, `p95_ms_ci95_total`, `ci_components`, `c1_capacity_ops_s`, `target_offered_ops_s`, `runs_used`, `runs_rejected`, `deployment_specific`, `reason`.

## 5. Resources, risks, rollback
- Cluster: context `kubernetes-admin@kubernetes`, namespace `qrl-calib` only (hard-coded; `default` is never touched; one node, 40 cpus, 201 GB). Pods: at most 9 in the main spec (17 in the optional one), each 50-200m CPU and 16Mi memory limit: at most 1.8 cores of pods.
- Host: load generator up to 16 worker processes (stage A approval said <= 8: THIS NEEDS APPROVAL), up to about 14 cores at the c=2/200m sweeps (16 workers, budget 358k ops/s) and up to about 13 cores at c = 8-9/50m. The VM also runs the single-node control plane (and whatever else the researcher runs there); host busy > 60 % marks a level invalid but does not stop it.
- Memory risk: pods at 16Mi limit idle at about 0.65 working set; the largest dataset is 2.2 MB in total, at most 1.1 MB per pod at 2 pods (about +0.07 of 16Mi: expected post-fill working set about 0.72, below the 0.85 start gate with margin 0.13, also during migration where a pod holds at most its own share plus in-flight keys). The test suite enforces <= 1.2 MB per pod for every entry. A trip (> 0.95) stops all scaling and triggers `reconcile.py`; the start gate refuses above 0.85.
- Data loss: plain scale-in may remove a master and its slots (the measurement); namespace data loss was accepted (R-20261016-018).
- Failure behaviour: every entry runs under `gate.py`; a failed entry triggers `rollback.sh` (detached) and every later entry refuses. `capacity-auto` entries depend on earlier sweeps of the same tag; a failed sweep therefore ends the run, as in stage A.
- Rollback: `bash code/calibration/rollback.sh` = `kubectl --context kubernetes-admin@kubernetes delete namespace qrl-calib` (the namespace only); the main spec deletes the namespace between the two halves and at the end.
- No `kubectl scale/patch/edit` appears in any spec entry; replica changes happen inside `scale_to.py` (tracked, as in stage A). Only the entries listed in the spec run.
- Order matters (data left behind in the namespace): datasets only grow within a namespace, so the large-value entries come last in each half.
